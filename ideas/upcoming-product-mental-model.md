## 2. Ayush's mental model (seen so far, Nov 2026)

Each point says what was seen and an example.

### How he plans
- **Goal and deadline first, then work backwards.** Example: "weekend tak yeh sab krna hai so that Monday se mail bheje." He names the end result and the date, and expects the steps to be worked out.
- **Research before build.** He often says "tu research kr bas abhi", "aaj sirf research". Understand the market and the options first, build later.
- **Big batched asks.** One message holds the whole vision (dashboard + priority + mocks + chance % + resume + project mining). He thinks in full systems, not single tasks.
- **No gaps.** He wants to be "fully ready" before facing anyone (interviews, case studies).

### How he gets work done (delegation style)
- **Trusts and hands over.** Gives the direction and leaves; expects Claude to figure out details from what already exists ("Baaki portfolio mai sab hai"). Does not want to be asked things that can be found.
- **Wants control on outward actions.** Never apply or send mail without asking him. Internal work (research, files, merges to his own repo) can go ahead.
- **Everything saved.** The find-jobs repo is his memory. Every step, data point and raw source must be written down and merged.
- **Learns while doing.** Wants Claude as a teacher, easiest way, and wants each project to also be a learning project and interview story.

### How he wants output
- Replies in simple Hinglish; documents in plain simple English that does not sound AI-written.
- Real numbers and impact over generic claims.
- Accuracy on facts about himself (for example: always use total experience across all companies).

### How he prompts (patterns in his prompts)
- Long single message, Hinglish, voice-like flow, few punctuation marks.
- States: the goal, the deadline, the pieces he imagines, who does what ("tu mock interviews banayega", "mai tuje project bta dunga"), and the current phase limit ("research kr bas abhi").
- Adds new ideas mid-flow as they come ("and one more thing...").
